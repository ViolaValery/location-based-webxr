Make your own tour

Put stories where they happened.

A tour can be as simple as a short walk with notes and photos - with more to come. Visitors scan the code you printed and hung, and your tour opens right in their phone's browser, with nothing to install.

  • Lives in your own storage
  • No app to install
  • Runs offline once opened
  • No servers of ours, so we never see your or your visitors' data

Six steps, then your walk.

Mill Park an example route
1 Step 1 of 6

  1. Where will your tour live?

    Your tour is one small zip file. Keep it in storage you already have: your visitors' browsers download it directly from there, and nothing of ours sits in between.

      Already have a tour zip on this device?

    1. Paste your tour's share link

      Paste the share link to your tour zip. It is checked by itself; the printed code will carry exactly this link, so keep the file where it is.

      A shortened link makes the printed code coarser and easier to scan from afar. Not every shortener works: some, bit.ly among them, open for you but stop the tour loading for visitors, so opening the short link yourself proves nothing. Paste the short link here and wait for the tour to be found before you print.

    2. Print your posters

      The code carries the link from step 2 - you are not asked for it again. Print it at 100% scale (no fit-to-page), and enter the printed side length so the AR setup can measure it.

      That is the address the code encodes: this page, carrying your tour link inside it. A shorter tour link means fewer, bigger squares (see step 2).

      Try this tour as a visitor on this phone

    3. Hang it where the tour starts

      Fix the printed code where the tour starts: flat on a wall or a post, at about chest height, in a spot a phone can film squarely from a metre away. It stays there; visitors scan it to open the tour and again in AR to be placed exactly.

    Create it on site, in AR

    Now the tour itself: stand at the hung code, start creating, and walk the route, placing text pins and photos where they belong. The phone measures the code as you begin and lines the tour up with GPS as you walk. When you are done, the tour file is saved to this phone, and the last step shows how to put it in place.

    The recording holds the tour link, your GPS track and the 3D shape of the surroundings.

    AR opens the camera. Keep the printed code in view until the page says it is measured; walking a few metres with GPS reception lets the phone align to the map.
